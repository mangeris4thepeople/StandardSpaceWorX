The Work

One truck. Three paychecks.

Every debris removal company before us built a vehicle that dies after one job. We build servicers that live in orbit, work a route, and earn three ways at once.

REVENUE 01

Debris Removal

Each servicer is locked to one orbital plane, like a crew assigned to one job site. Park dormant, wake on tasking, phase to the target, capture, dispose, return.

Reusing one vehicle across dozens of targets cuts removal cost from the industry's 90 million dollar class to the 3 to 8 million dollar class.

REVENUE 02

Earth Observation Data

Our home plane is sun synchronous orbit, the same ground lighting on every pass, the most valuable imaging orbit in space. Between jobs, the servicer's camera points down and collects imagery for sale.

The camera is ours alone. We never sell imaging or recording space on the vessel; the data business stays in house.

REVENUE 03

Hosted Comms Payloads

Spare mass, power, and pointing on each servicer is leased to communications tenants: transmitters and receivers that ride our vessel and pay rent, the way carriers lease space on a cell tower.

Transmit and receive hardware only. No cameras, no recorders, no exceptions. Tenants get the ride; the view stays ours.

Why Now

The 2027 to 2030 convergence

Regulation with teeth

The FCC five year disposal rule is enforced and the first fine has been levied. ESA requires 90 percent disposal success. Insurers now ask for debris compliance before writing policies.

Tenants are moving in

Multiple gigawatt scale orbital data center constellations have filed for sun synchronous orbit, the same band the worst debris contaminates. Somebody has to clear the site before they move in.

Launch costs are collapsing

Next generation launch pricing makes a standardized, mass produced servicer fleet affordable for the first time in history. Rideshare to our first plane is already routine.
