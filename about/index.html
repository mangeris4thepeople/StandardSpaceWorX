About

Standard SpaceWorX: an aerospace company in Loveland, Colorado

Standard SpaceWorX is a space debris removal venture of Standard Data Company. We design reusable servicing satellites, called Terriers, that capture orbital debris and return to a parked orbit for the next job. We are based in Loveland, Colorado, on the Front Range, and we serve satellite operators, government and defense buyers, aerospace partners, and investors across the United States.

What we do

The company exists to make active debris removal a repeatable service. Everything else follows from that. We build vehicles that are reused rather than consumed, we lock each vehicle to one orbital plane and work that plane until it is clear, and we design the vehicle to earn imaging and hosted payload revenue between removals so a fleet can be financed before the removal market is fully priced. The vehicle is the Terrier. The reasoning is on the mission page.

Standard Data Company

Standard Data Company is a Loveland, Colorado S corporation. It is the parent of Standard SpaceWorX and holds all Standard SpaceWorX intellectual property, including the satellite design reference package and the patent landscape study. Standard Data Company also operates civic data infrastructure and custom software work, which is where the company's habit of publishing real numbers and building in the open comes from. Standard SpaceWorX is operated as a distinct venture with its own brand, channels, and investor relationships, and is structured so that it can be capitalized independently.

Leadership

Doug Mangeris, Founder and Chief Executive Officer

Doug Mangeris founded Standard SpaceWorX and leads it. His career is in field operations and large capital projects. As a commercial construction superintendent he has delivered more than 230 million dollars of work across healthcare, multifamily, senior living, retail, and industrial projects for general contractors including Hausmann Construction, Brinkmann Constructors, TCC Corporation, and Colorado Concepts. Before construction he spent four years in upstream oil and gas drilling with Cyclone Drilling, Helmerich and Payne, and Cade Drilling, and holds WellCAP master driller certification. He holds the ICC National Standard General Building Contractor (A) certification and MSHA and OSHA 40 hour certifications, and a bachelor's degree in History from the University of Nevada, Las Vegas.

That background is deliberate for this company. Running a job site means sequencing crews, equipment, and materials so that expensive assets are never idle and every task follows a known procedure. A debris removal fleet is the same problem in a harder environment: a small number of expensive vehicles, a long list of targets, and a requirement that every capture follow a repeatable playbook. The Terrier program is run as a fleet operations problem with a hard engineering core.

Doug serves as Vice President on the board of Habitat for Humanity of Loveland. He has a long-standing interest in civic accountability and data-driven decision making, which is reflected in how Standard SpaceWorX publishes its design reference values rather than keeping them vague.

How we work

  • Numbers first. We publish our design reference values and the thresholds we are designing against, and we update them as the program matures.
  • Retire the hardest risk first. The capture of a tumbling, uncooperative object is the hardest step in the mission cycle, so it is the first thing we prototype, on the ground.
  • Standardize. A finite set of target classes, approach geometries, and capture procedures, so the fortieth mission costs less than the first.
  • Keep the sensors. Imaging on our vehicles is ours. Hosted payloads are transmit and receive hardware only.
  • Build domestic capability. We are a United States company operating from Colorado and we design for government and defense buyers who need a domestic removal option.

Where we are

Standard SpaceWorX operates from Loveland, Colorado, in Larimer County on the northern Front Range. Loveland sits between Fort Collins, home of Colorado State University, and the Boulder and Denver aerospace clusters, and within reach of the Space Force installations in Colorado Springs and Aurora. Colorado has one of the largest aerospace economies in the country, which is why the company is here: the propulsion, guidance, mechanisms, and operations talent the Terrier program needs lives within an hour's drive. We are a service-area business and do not operate a public walk-in office. Partners and investors are welcome to meet in Loveland, Fort Collins, Boulder, or Denver.

Who we serve

Satellite operators who need an object moved or a disposal partner. Government and defense buyers with catalog objects of concern. Aerospace partners in launch, propulsion, sensors, and ground operations. And investors who want exposure to on-orbit servicing through a model with recurring revenue.
