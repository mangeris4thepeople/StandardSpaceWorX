Why the Front Range is the right place to build a debris removal company

Standard SpaceWorX is based in Loveland, Colorado. That is not an accident of where the founder lives. It is where the talent, the customers, and the launch and operations partners for a reusable servicer program already are.

Colorado's aerospace economy

Colorado has one of the largest aerospace economies in the United States and, by most measures, the largest per capita. The industry here spans the full stack: prime contractors building spacecraft and launch vehicles, satellite manufacturers and operators, Earth observation companies, component and propulsion suppliers, and the military space enterprise. Lockheed Martin Space is headquartered in the Denver suburbs. United Launch Alliance is based in Centennial. Boulder and Broomfield host major satellite and instrument manufacturers along with the Laboratory for Atmospheric and Space Physics at the University of Colorado, which has flown instruments on missions to every planet. Newer entrants in small satellite manufacturing, Earth observation, and in-space logistics have clustered in Denver, Westminster, Louisville, and Broomfield over the last decade.

The military and government footprint

Colorado Springs hosts United States Space Command and several Space Force installations, including Peterson and Schriever Space Force Bases. Buckley Space Force Base sits in Aurora on the east side of Denver. Space Operations Command and the units that track the orbital catalog and manage conjunction warnings are here. For a company whose product is removing objects from that catalog, and whose government and defense customers are the organizations that maintain it, being within a two hour drive of those buyers is a practical advantage, not a line on a slide.

The university pipeline

The University of Colorado Boulder runs one of the largest aerospace engineering programs in the country and has a long record of building and operating flight hardware through LASP. Colorado State University in Fort Collins, fifteen minutes from Loveland, produces mechanical, electrical, and systems engineers and has growing space and atmospheric science programs. The Colorado School of Mines in Golden is building a space resources program. Between them, the Front Range graduates spacecraft engineers every year, and a meaningful share of them want to stay in Colorado. For an early stage company whose first hires are propulsion, guidance and navigation, and mechanisms engineers, that pipeline is the single most important resource.

What Northern Colorado specifically offers

Loveland sits in Larimer County on the northern end of the Front Range, between Fort Collins and the Boulder and Denver clusters. Northern Colorado has an established precision manufacturing base, including aerospace component suppliers, and a cost of operating that is lower than Boulder or Denver while remaining within commuting distance of both. The Northern Colorado Regional Airport is in Loveland. For a company that will build and test ground prototype hardware before it builds flight units, access to machine shops, test facilities, and engineers who want to live near the mountains rather than in a metro core is a real advantage.

Why this matters to investors

Location shows up in three places on a cap table. First, hiring: the engineers a prototype program needs are here and do not have to be relocated. Second, customer access: the government buyers who maintain the catalog and the commercial operators who need objects moved are within driving distance. Third, cost: Northern Colorado lets an early stage company run lean while staying inside the ecosystem. For investors looking at space startup investment in Colorado specifically, the Front Range is one of a small number of places in the country where a spacecraft company can be built end to end without leaving the state.
